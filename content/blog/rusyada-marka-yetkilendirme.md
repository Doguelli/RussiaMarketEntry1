  Marka Yetkilendirme Nedir?


  Marka yetkilendirmesi, marka sahibinin Rusya'daki bir şirket veya iş ortağına belirli koşullar altında markayı temsil etme, ürünlerini satma veya marka ile ilgili belirli ticari faaliyetleri yürütme yetkisi vermesidir.

  Markayı temsil etmek ile markanın kullanım hakkını devretmek aynı şey değildir.

  Ürünlerini satma,

  Marketplace'te satış yapma,

  Markayı tanıtma,

  Pazarlama faaliyetleri yürütme,

  Distribütörlük yapma,

  Belirli bölgelerde temsil etme


  gibi farklı yetkiler verebilir.


  Bu yetkilerin kapsamı sözleşmede açık şekilde tanımlanmalıdır.

  # Brand Authorization in Russia: A Guide for Turkish Brands


  ### Brand Authorization Process, Distribution Agreements and Marketplace Requirements


  For Turkish brands entering the Russian market, shipping products to Russia and listing them on a marketplace is only one part of the market entry process.


  It is equally important to determine **who will represent the brand in Russia, who will be authorized to sell its products, and under what conditions the brand can be used.**


  This becomes particularly important when selling through marketplaces such as **Wildberries and Ozon**, where sellers may be required to provide documents proving their relationship with the brand and the authenticity of the products.


  For this reason, **brand authorization agreements, distribution agreements and, where necessary, licensing agreements** should be considered from the beginning of the market entry process.


  In this guide, we explain the key aspects of **brand authorization in Russia**, the different agreement structures available to Turkish brands, and the points that should be considered before starting sales.


  ---


  ## What Is Brand Authorization?


  Brand authorization is the process through which a brand owner grants a company or business partner in Russia specific rights to represent, sell or use the brand within defined commercial conditions.


  However, an important distinction should be made:


  **Representing a brand and granting the right to use a trademark are not necessarily the same thing.**


  A Turkish brand owner may grant its Russian partner the right to:


  * Sell its products

  * Sell products on marketplaces

  * Promote the brand

  * Conduct marketing activities

  * Act as a distributor

  * Represent the brand within a specific territory

  * Use approved brand materials


  The exact scope of these rights should be clearly defined in the agreement.


  ---


  # Why Is Brand Authorization Important When Entering Russia?


  An incomplete or unclear documentation structure can create problems during the Russian sales operation, particularly on marketplaces.


  For example, a seller may state:


  > "We purchase these products directly from the manufacturer in Turkey."


  But from a marketplace perspective, another question may be more important:


  > **"How can you prove that you are authorized to sell and use this brand?"**


  Wildberries distinguishes between different statuses such as brand owner, licensee, distributor, dealer and reseller, and may request documents proving the seller's relationship with the brand and the authenticity of the products.


  Therefore, brand authorization should not be treated as a document to prepare **after entering the Russian market**.


  It should be considered as part of the **market entry strategy from the beginning.**


  ---


  # Is Brand Authorization the Same as a Distribution Agreement?


  No.


  Although these concepts are often used together, they can serve different commercial and legal purposes.


  ## Brand Authorization


  Brand authorization gives a company specific rights to represent or use a brand for defined purposes.


  For example:


  > "Company X is an authorized representative of our brand in the Russian Federation."


  The agreement may define exactly what this authorization covers.


  ## Distribution Agreement


  A distributor may take responsibility for a broader commercial operation, including:


  * Purchasing products

  * Importing products

  * Distributing products

  * Selling products

  * Managing sales channels

  * Maintaining inventory

  * Meeting sales targets


  A distribution relationship may therefore involve:


  **Product supply + sales + distribution + commercial targets**


  However, even where a distributor is appointed, the rights to use the brand should be clearly addressed in the contractual structure.


  ---


  # Brand Authorization Models for the Russian Market


  There is no single model that works for every brand.


  The appropriate structure depends on the Turkish manufacturer's business model and Russian market strategy.


  ## 1. Authorized Reseller Model


  A Russian company purchases the products from the brand and sells them through agreed channels.


  For example:


  **Turkish Brand**


  ↓


  **Authorized Russian Reseller**


  ↓


  **Wildberries / Ozon**


  This model can be particularly practical when starting marketplace sales.


  ---


  ## 2. Distribution Model


  A Russian partner can be appointed as a distributor for a specific territory or sales channel.


  The agreement may define:


  * Territory

  * Sales channels

  * Minimum purchase quantities

  * Sales targets

  * Marketing responsibilities

  * Pricing policies

  * Inventory responsibilities

  * Reporting requirements


  This creates a clearer commercial framework between the manufacturer and the Russian distributor.


  ---


  ## 3. Exclusive Distribution


  A brand owner may appoint one partner as its **exclusive distributor** in Russia.


  However, this decision requires careful consideration.


  Exclusivity can effectively mean:


  > "Only this partner is authorized to sell the brand within the agreed territory or channel."


  Therefore, exclusivity should ideally be defined together with:


  **Territory + duration + sales channels + performance targets**


  This allows the brand owner to maintain greater control over the relationship.


  ---


  ## 4. Non-Exclusive Authorization


  The brand owner may work with several sellers or distributors in the same market.


  For example:


  **Russia**


  → Wildberries partner

  → Ozon partner

  → B2B distributor

  → Authorized dealers


  This model can provide greater flexibility, particularly for brands entering Russia for the first time and testing the market.

  # What Should a Brand Authorization Agreement Include?


  A strong authorization agreement should not simply state:


  > "This company is authorized to sell our products in Russia."


  The scope of the authorization should be clearly defined.


  ## 1. Parties


  The agreement should identify:


  * Brand owner

  * Authorized company

  * Company registration details

  * Tax or registration information

  * Authorized representatives


  ---


  ## 2. Brand Information


  The agreement should clearly identify which brands are covered.


  Where appropriate, it may include:


  * Brand name

  * Logo

  * Trademark registration details

  * Product categories

  * Relevant product lines


  The trademark's legal status in Russia should also be reviewed separately.


  ---


  # 3. Scope of Authorization


  This is one of the most important sections.


  Can the authorized company:


  **Use the brand name?**


  **Use the logo?**


  **Use product images?**


  **Create marketplace listings?**


  **Run advertising campaigns?**


  **Create social media accounts?**


  **Use the brand in promotional materials?**


  All of these rights should be clearly defined.


  ---


  # 4. Territory


  The authorization can cover:


  **The entire Russian Federation**


  or specific territories and/or sales channels.


  The geographical scope should be clearly stated in the agreement.


  ---


  # 5. Sales Channels


  This is particularly important for marketplace operations.


  Authorization may cover:


  * Wildberries

  * Ozon

  * Lamoda

  * Brand's own website

  * B2B sales

  * Offline retail


  For example, one partner may be authorized specifically for Wildberries while another manages Ozon.


  ---


  # 6. Agreement Duration


  Authorization does not necessarily need to be indefinite.


  The agreement may specify:


  **12 months**


  **24 months**


  or another defined period.


  Renewal conditions should also be clearly established.


  ---


  # 7. Exclusivity


  If the partner is appointed as an exclusive distributor, exclusivity should be explicitly defined.


  For example:


  > **Exclusive distributor of the brand in the Russian Federation.**


  However, it can also be useful to include performance conditions such as:


  **Minimum annual sales target**


  This helps protect the brand owner from granting exclusivity without receiving sufficient commercial performance in return.


  ---


  # 8. Marketplace Authorization


  This has become particularly important for brands selling in Russia.


  Wildberries may require documentation demonstrating the seller's relationship with a brand, depending on whether the seller is a brand owner, licensee, distributor, dealer or another type of authorized seller.


  For licensees and representatives, documents such as licensing agreements or powers of attorney may be relevant.


  Therefore, the agreement should explicitly address whether the authorized party may use the brand on marketplaces such as:


  **Wildberries and Ozon.**


  This can significantly simplify the operational process when marketplace documentation is requested.


  ---


  # 9. Marketing and Advertising Rights


  The agreement can also define whether the Russian partner is authorized to use the brand in:


  * Yandex

  * VK

  * Telegram

  * Marketplace advertising

  * Influencer marketing

  * YouTube

  * Offline advertising


  The brand owner may also establish an approval process for advertising materials and visual assets.


  ---


  # 10. Brand Standards


  The Russian market communication should remain consistent with the brand's identity.


  The agreement or an accompanying brand guideline may therefore define:


  * Logo usage

  * Visual standards

  * Product photography

  * Advertising language

  * Brand messaging

  * Packaging presentation

  * Promotional materials


  This becomes especially important when several Russian partners are operating simultaneously.


  ---


  # 11. Sub-Distribution and Transfer of Rights


  If the authorized distributor should not be allowed to transfer its rights to another company, this should be explicitly stated.


  For example:


  > **Appointment of a sub-distributor or granting of a sub-license requires the prior written approval of the brand owner.**


  This helps the brand owner maintain control over how its rights are used in the Russian market.


  ---


  # 12. Termination


  The agreement should also define what happens when the relationship ends.


  For example:


  * Marketplace accounts

  * Brand assets

  * Advertising accounts

  * Social media accounts

  * Remaining inventory

  * Brand usage rights

  * Existing orders

  * Marketing materials


  All of these should be addressed as part of the exit strategy.

  # Should Turkish Brands Register Their Trademark in Russia?


  This is an important distinction.


  **A trademark registered in Turkey does not automatically provide the same level of protection in Russia.**


  Trademark protection in Russia should therefore be assessed separately.


  For Turkish brands planning a long-term presence in Russia, the following sequence should be considered:


  **Trademark research → Trademark protection → Authorization → Marketplace**


  This becomes particularly important before investing heavily in marketing, marketplace operations and distribution.

  # Why Is Brand Authorization Important on Wildberries?


  Wildberries may request documentation proving the seller's relationship with a brand and the authenticity of products.

  * Distribution or dealer agreements

  * Invoices

  * Shipping documents

  * Import documents

  * Trademark documentation

  * Licensing agreements

  * Powers of attorney


  This means that simply saying:


  > **"We purchased these products from the manufacturer in Turkey."**

  There is an important difference between:


  **Proving that a product is authentic**


  and


  **Proving that the seller has the right to use and represent the brand.**

  # Common Mistakes Turkish Brands Make


  ### ❌ "We have a distribution agreement, so no separate brand authorization is necessary."


  Not every distribution agreement automatically provides the same scope of trademark usage rights. The agreement should be reviewed carefully.


  ### ❌ "Our Turkish trademark registration is enough."


  Trademark protection in Russia should be assessed separately.


  ### ❌ "Marketplaces do not ask for brand documents."


  Marketplaces may request documentation proving brand rights and product authenticity.


  ### ❌ "Let's give the partner exclusivity and discuss sales targets later."


  If exclusivity is granted, performance conditions should ideally be defined at the same time.


  ### ❌ "We appointed a distributor, so they control everything in Russia."


  The brand owner should determine from the beginning how much control it wants over pricing, marketing, marketplace operations, brand communication and customer experience.
